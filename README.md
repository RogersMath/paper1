## Key Findings Summary

1. **Bloom's Two Sigma Problem has never been replicated**: The d = 2.0 effect size was methodologically flawed and combined multiple interventions beyond tutoring
2. **Modern AI tutors achieve human-level effectiveness**: Effect sizes of d = 0.4-0.8, comparable to human tutoring at lower cost
3. **Pedagogical design matters more than AI capability**: Guardrails and Socratic questioning outperform more advanced unstructured systems
4. **Evidence-based techniques remain underutilized**: Self-explanation, elaborative interrogation, and metacognitive scaffolding show strong research support but weak implementation
5. **Hybrid models show promise**: AI augmenting human tutors (e.g., Stanford's Tutor CoPilot) produces better outcomes than pure replacement

## Suggested Future Research Directions

### 1. Long-Term Transfer Effects of AI Tutoring

**Research Question**: Do students who use AI tutoring systems for extended periods (>6 months) maintain learning gains when AI support is removed, or does the "crutch effect" persist across longer timeframes?

**Rationale**: The 2025 PNAS study documented short-term dependency, but we lack evidence about whether students develop self-regulation skills over time or remain persistently reliant on AI scaffolding. This has critical implications for curriculum design.

**Methodology**: Longitudinal RCT with periodic "AI-off" assessment phases, measuring both content mastery and self-regulated learning behaviors across a full academic year.

### 2. Optimal Integration Ratios for Human-AI Tutoring

**Research Question**: What proportion of tutoring time should involve AI assistance versus pure human interaction to maximize both learning outcomes and cost-effectiveness while preserving relational benefits?

**Rationale**: The Stanford Tutor CoPilot showed AI augmentation working well, but we don't know the optimal "dose" of AI involvement. Too little may waste technological potential; too much may erode the human connection that benefits student motivation and persistence.

**Methodology**: Multi-armed trial comparing 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% AI involvement in tutoring sessions, measuring academic outcomes, student engagement, tutor satisfaction, and cost per achievement gain.

### 3. Implementation of Metacognitive Scaffolding in LLM-Based Tutors

**Research Question**: Can systematic metacognitive prompting embedded in AI tutors improve students' self-regulated learning and reduce dependency compared to content-focused AI tutoring alone?

**Rationale**: Current AI tutors excel at content delivery but rarely teach students *how to learn*. Explicit metacognitive instruction (planning, monitoring, evaluation) could address the self-regulation decline documented by Xu (2025) while building transferable learning skills.

**Methodology**: Design-based research comparing three conditions: (a) standard AI tutor, (b) AI tutor with metacognitive prompts, (c) AI tutor with metacognitive prompts plus explicit strategy instruction. Measure both content learning and self-regulated learning survey instruments.
